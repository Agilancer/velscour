# The Veilscar March — Area Vision

## The surface wound

The Veilscar March is the overworld scar surrounding the Eternal Breach. A once-inhabited upland has been split by a breach that never closes. The land does not become a generic lava field: it remains recognizably a grave-country of heath, processional roads, burial mounds, broken chapels, wind-cut stone, and dead root networks. The rift has altered those familiar things from within.

The color story begins with charcoal earth, weathered limestone, ash-brown grass, dead umber roots, and restrained bone. Purple appears as a clear secondary accent: violet glass in cracks, purple lichen along stone edges, rift-lit roots, and narrow amethyst trim on memorials and structures. Small pools and exposed seams glow more brightly; broad terrain stays subdued so the trim reads at sprite scale.

## Visual language

Keep the established 2:1 dimetric ground plane and compact pixel-art clusters. Terrain should read first as a clean walkable surface, then as material variation: chipped slate, heather ash, grave moss, bone dust, and root-split paving. Purple accents follow seams, edges, and inlays instead of filling large flat areas. Architecture uses dark iron, aged limestone, and wood stained by ash. Scatter silhouettes should remain distinct from the floor and cast their visual weight toward the shared ground plane.

## Area progression

1. **Ashen approach:** heath loam and old processional paths, sparse grave markers, and isolated violet seams.
2. **Rootsplit burial fields:** exposed root lattices, broken flagstones, cairns, and collapsed memorials threaded with purple glass.
3. **Veilscar margin:** darkened ground, twisted root growth, crystal clusters, sealed entrances, and increasingly frequent violet trim.
4. **The breach lip:** fractured masonry, ritual fixtures, and a visible transition into the deeper Eternal Breach palette.

## Play-space cues

Keep the main paths legible and reserve the brightest violet for points of interest, dangerous seams, and interactable rift growth. Use scattered remains and root clusters to frame routes without hiding tile boundaries. The paired biome should feel like the same world as the catacombs, with the surface's wind, soil, roots, and weather still visible around the dungeon's stonework.

## Asset scope

The Veilscar March pack includes 60 floors, five modular wall families, three arches, three doors with open and closed states, and 40 scatter props. It uses the same 160×80 tile basis and 236-frame packaging layout as The Eternal Breach, so both zones can be assembled with the same placement workflow.
