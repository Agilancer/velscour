## Patterns carried into the map

- Great halls center attention on a dais, hearth, or raised seat while keeping the middle of the room open. Furniture and wall detail cluster at the sides, preserving a clear floor for gatherings and movement.
- Chapels and castle churches use a legible nave: an unobstructed center aisle, repeated pew rows, a raised pulpit or altar, and vertical light from candles or hanging fixtures.
- Keep interiors need distinct circulation and thresholds. Thick wall lines, door openings, stair landings, and short links between chambers help the rooms feel like one defended building rather than props placed in an outdoor yard.
- Interior materials vary by room and period. The map uses a cooler dressed-stone throne hall, a lighter chapel floor with warm masonry, and a darker brick great hall so the rooms read separately while remaining within the same Castle palette.

The reference variety includes defensive stone keeps, ceremonial royal halls, restored rooms, and later castle-palace interiors. Representative interior views include [Warwick Castle's Great Hall](https://www.warwick-castle.com/explore/events/castle-ceilidh/), [Bamburgh Castle's King's Hall](https://www.visitnorthumberland.com/explore/things-to-do/attractions/historic-sites/bamburgh-castle), [the Great Hall at Kronborg](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/696/), [Cardiff Castle's interiors](https://www.cardiffcastle.com/explore/interiors/), [Conwy Castle](https://cadw.gov.wales/visit/places-to-visit/castell-conwy), and [Himeji Castle](https://www.nippon.com/en/guide-to-japan/gu900254/).
